## The Huntsville Market: Two Economies, One Zip Code

Most towns along the I-45 corridor have a single, simple growth story: Houston expands, rooftops follow, businesses follow the rooftops. Huntsville's story is different, and it is more interesting. You have Sam Houston State University bringing in tens of thousands of students, faculty, and staff every fall, spring, and (to a lesser degree) summer. Those students need housing, food, entertainment, tutoring, phone repair, haircuts, and everything else a young, mobile population needs. Then you have the Texas Department of Criminal Justice, headquartered right here, employing a large, stable workforce of state employees and their families who are not going anywhere when the semester ends or a term ends. And layered on top of both of those, you have a historic downtown square that draws tourists and history buffs in to see the Sam Houston statue, visit the Sam Houston Memorial Museum, or walk through the Texas Prison Museum on their way to or from Huntsville State Park.

That is three different customer bases with three different rhythms, all shopping in the same small city. A restaurant near the SHSU campus lives and dies by the academic calendar, with a completely different summer than the rest of the year. A professional services firm serving TDCJ employees and their families operates on a steady, government-paycheck rhythm that does not spike and crash the way a college-town business does. A gift shop or diner on the downtown square is trying to catch a tourist who might be in town for an afternoon and never come back. If your website does not know which of these customers it is talking to, it is not doing its job.

A lot of Huntsville businesses have not thought about this at all. They have a website, if they have one, that was built once and never touched again. It says nothing about hours around finals week, nothing about parking near the square during a museum visit, nothing that would help a new TDCJ employee's spouse, just relocated from Huntsville's satellite housing near Westwood or Raven Hills, figure out where to get a haircut or find a dentist. That is not a design problem. That is a strategy problem, and it is exactly what I build for.
