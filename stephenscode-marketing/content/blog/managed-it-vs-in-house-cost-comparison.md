## The Conversation I Have With Almost Every Growing Houston Business

At some point between 10 and 50 employees, every business owner I talk to has the same realization: IT stopped being a side task and started being a real problem. Somebody's cousin who's "good with computers" isn't cutting it anymore. The one person who understands the network is also the person who can never take a real vacation. A vendor renewal gets missed, a laptop gets stolen with no backup, or a phishing email almost gets a wire transfer approved, and suddenly the question isn't "should we do something about IT" anymore. It's "what do we actually do."

The two paths people consider are hiring an in-house IT person (or building a small internal team) or bringing on a managed service provider, an MSP, to handle it. I get asked to compare these constantly, so here's the honest version, with real numbers, not a sales pitch dressed up as an article.

I'll say upfront that we run managed IT services ourselves through our [MSP division](/msp), so you should read the numbers below and decide for yourself. I'm going to give you the industry ranges first, unfiltered, before I ever mention our own pricing.

## What an In-House IT Hire Actually Costs

The number people fixate on is the salary line, and that number alone is already misleading. Nationally, a single IT support or generalist hire runs somewhere between $45,000 a year for an entry-level helpdesk role and $75,000 or more for a senior generalist or sysadmin, based on the salary aggregators (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Glassdoor, Payscale) that track this. In the Houston market specifically, a qualified systems administrator commonly runs $75,000 to $105,000 a year in base salary, higher than the national floor because Houston competes with the energy and medical sectors for the same technical talent.

That's just the base. Employers should budget roughly 1.25x to 1.45x base salary once you add payroll taxes, benefits (health, dental, vision, PTO, 401k), and overhead like equipment, software licensing, training, and recruiting. Federal labor data backs this up in the aggregate: private employers were spending around $13.68 an hour in benefits on top of $32.37 an hour in wages as of late 2025, meaning benefits alone run about 42 cents for every dollar of wages.

Put that together and a single in-house IT hire in Houston has a fully-loaded true cost that typically lands around $95,000 to $137,000 a year, once you're honest about everything beyond the salary line. And here's the part that actually matters for a 10-50 employee business: that number buys you exactly one person. No redundancy. No bench strength. No coverage when they're sick, on vacation, or simply off the clock at 9pm when a server goes down.

## What Managed IT Services Actually Cost

MSP pricing nationally runs a wide range, commonly cited as $100 to $300 per user per month depending on scope, with small businesses in the 10-50 employee range most often landing in the $100 to $175 per user band. A frequently cited "typical comprehensive" average sits around $150 to $200 per user per month. Houston-specific MSP marketing pages tend to quote somewhere in the $100 to $250 per user range, depending on what's bundled in.

The tiering pattern shows up consistently across the industry:

- **Basic** (helpdesk plus monitoring only): roughly $100-125 per user
- **Standard** (adds backup, cybersecurity tooling, cloud management): roughly $150-200 per user
- **Premium** (adds compliance support, 24/7 SOC, virtual CIO): roughly $200-300 per user

For a 25-person company, that works out to somewhere between $2,500 and $7,500 a month total, scaling directly with headcount, versus a single in-house hire's $95,000-$137,000 a year that stays fixed whether that person is fully utilized or sitting idle half the week.

One honest caveat worth flagging, because too many articles about MSP pricing skip it: contracts can carry hidden add-on costs. After-hours emergency rates, project work that falls outside the standard scope, and extra licensing can inflate the quoted per-user rate by another 30 to 50 percent in practice. If an MSP quote looks suspiciously clean, ask what's not included before you sign anything. ## Where the Break-Even Point Actually Falls

This is the part most people get wrong in either direction. Houston-market data converges on a fairly consistent pattern: under about 50 employees, an MSP almost always wins on both cost and coverage breadth. The gray zone, a hybrid or "co-managed" model with one internal generalist plus an MSP for overflow, security, and after-hours coverage, tends to become competitive somewhere in the 50-200 employee range. A fully in-house IT department typically doesn't pencil out until roughly 150-200+ employees, and even past that threshold, most companies keep some co-managed structure rather than going 100 percent internal.

If you're running a 10-50 employee business, you are squarely in the range where an MSP, or at most a co-managed model, is the cost-rational choice. A dedicated, fully in-house IT department usually doesn't break even until your company is 3-4 times its current headcount.

## The Real Risk Isn't the Cost. It's the "One IT Guy" Model.

Cost is only half the argument, and honestly it's not even the half that should worry you most. The bigger risk with a single internal IT hire is structural.

**Single point of failure.** When one person holds all the institutional knowledge, passwords, network configuration, vendor relationships, their departure, illness, or even just a bad week can leave your systems undocumented and unsupported. I've seen it happen: passwords that only ever lived in a departed employee's personal password manager, and nobody left who knows which vendor to even call.

**No real coverage.** A single hire can't take a vacation, get sick, or go off-call without leaving the business exposed. Nothing gets watched or patched while they're out. That same pressure is also what burns people out and eventually makes them quit, which restarts the entire hiring and knowledge-transfer risk from zero.

**Skill gaps that create delay.** One generalist can't realistically stay sharp across networking, cybersecurity, cloud administration, compliance, and end-user support at the same time. Security research has consistently tied longer detection and response times to environments where one person is stretched across every function instead of a team with defined coverage lanes. A gap in specialist knowledge doesn't announce itself. It just sits there until something exploits it.

None of this means the answer is firing your internal IT person if you have one. The standard, honest fix, the one that isn't just an MSP sales pitch, is pairing that person with external backup so there's redundancy, documentation, and after-hours coverage without doubling your headcount cost.
